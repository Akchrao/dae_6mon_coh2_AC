1.3 Conflicting requirements and how they are resolved

TopicConflicting requirementsResolution
Breach notification clockGDPR Art. 33: supervisory authority within 72 h of awareness; HIPAA 164.404/408: individuals and HHS without unreasonable delay, no later than 60 days (500+ people); DPDP Rules 2025: report to the Data Protection Board within 72 h; PCI DSS: notify acquirer / card brands immediatelyOne global incident clock set to the strictest rule: preliminary regulator-ready report within 72 h for every incident involving personal data, HIPAA and card-brand notices issued in parallel well inside 60 days. Clock starts at SOC triage (SOC 1 playbook).
Retention vs. erasureHIPAA 164.316(b)(2): keep compliance documentation 6 years; NY: adult medical records 6 years; Germany BGB 630f: patient records 10 years; GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) storage limitation and Art. 17 erasure; PCI DSS 10.5.1: audit logs 12 months; DPDP s.8(7): erase when the purpose is servedRetention schedule by data class AND jurisdiction of the record, never a single global maximum. Medical records follow the local law; security logs 12 months then deleted; GDPR erasure requests are refused only under Art. 17(3)(b)/(c) with the legal obligation cited.
Cross-border transfersGDPR Chapter V: EU personal data may leave the EU only with adequacy (US: EU-US Data Privacy Framework for certified recipients) or SCCs + transfer impact assessment; India has no EU adequacy decision; DPDP s.16: transfers allowed except to countries the Government restricts; HIPAA: no localisation, but BAAs requiredEU patient data stays in an EU tenant. Hyderabad staff work through VDI on pseudonymised billing data; SCCs + transfer impact assessment for EU-to-India access; BAAs for US data; no Indian patient data in scope.
Legal basis for processing health dataHIPAA: treatment, payment, operations without authorisation; GDPR Art. 9(2)(h): health care without consent but under professional secrecy; DPDP s.6-7: consent or a listed 'legitimate use'Purpose-and-basis register per region in CISO Assistant. No reliance on consent where GDPR/HIPAA give a care-delivery basis; consent notices only for Indian staff data and marketing.
Patients' and data subjects' access rightsHIPAA 164.524: 30 days (+30 extension); GDPR Art. 15: 1 month (+2 for complex); DPDP s.11: right to informationOne request process with a 30-day global target (meets both); extensions only where the local law allows, logged in the tracker.
Workforce monitoring vs. employee privacyPCI DSS 10 and HIPAA 164.312(b) require activity logging and review; GDPR Art. 88 and German BetrVG s.87(1) no. 6 give the works council co-determination over monitoring technologySIEM monitoring limited to security purposes; works-council agreement in Munich before Wazuh rules go live; pseudonymised analyst views in the EU, names revealed only on escalation (SOC 1 workflow).

Principle: where rules conflict on time, use the strictest (72 hours, 30 days); where they conflict on data, apply the law of the record's jurisdiction, never a global maximum; where they conflict on location, keep data in its region and move access, not data.

1.5 Enterprise compliance architecture (10,000+ staff, multiple locations)

Source: diagrams/compliance_architecture.mmd
LayerDesign for scale
GovernanceBoard committee → CCO → regional leads (US HIPAA officers, EU DPO, India lead) + CISO for PCI DSS. 43 compliance champions (1 per 250 staff) in departments.
ProcessOne global process per topic (risk, testing, audit, breach, DSAR, vendors) with regional rule tables, so a new site plugs into existing processes.
PlatformFederated identity (Entra ID + Keycloak) so 10,800 joiners/movers/leavers are automated; Wazuh for log evidence; CISO Assistant with one domain per region as the system of record; the workflow engine reads it continuously.
DataRegional residency: US data in the US, EU data in the EU; Hyderabad has no patient data at rest and works through VDI on pseudonymised billing data.
